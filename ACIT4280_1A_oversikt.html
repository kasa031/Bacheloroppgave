ACIT4280 Privacy by Design - group submission overview

Internal guide for the group. Deadline 3 September 2026, 08:00. 40 points.

What we did in one sentence: We measured how much 18 sites contact other companies on load (Webbkoll), and we checked whether five of them have a valid GDPR reason for one specific use of personal data (ICO tool).

What we deliver to Canvas: English report (1000-1500 words plus diagrams) and a 20-minute presentation (17 slides).

1. The two research questions

These questions sit on the title slide, in the report introduction, and in the presentation close:

  1. When a front page loads, how many other companies does it contact, and in which countries do those machines appear to sit?
  2. When a site uses personal data, has it named a GDPR Article 6 basis, and does the ICO tool agree?

Contact and lawfulness are different questions. A site can load many ad scripts and still have a lawful contract for checkout.

2. What the assignment asks for

Part 1 - Webbkoll (18 sites)

  • Table with server country, cookies, third-party requests and countries
  • Charts per sector
  • Who contacts the most, who looks most global, quietest e-government site

Part 2 - ICO (5 sites, group 1-A)

  • Read the privacy notice
  • Run the ICO tool on one purpose at a time
  • Compare notice vs ICO result in Table 5

3. Main findings (matches report, slides and conclusion)

Part 1 - what we looked at

  • Cookies - small files on the device
  • Third-party requests - the page calling other companies (ads, fonts, analytics)
  • Countries - KeyCDN guess from IP addresses (not a legal transfer register)

Key point: many requests does not mean many cookies.

Part 1 - the numbers that matter

  • Most requests: fotball.no (113) - but zero third-party cookies
  • Only third-party cookies: ikea.no (2, after redirect to ikea.com). 17 of 18 sites had zero.
  • Quietest government sites: lanekassen.no (1 request), altinn.no (5)
  • Widest country list: document.no (6 countries excluding Norway)
  • By sector: news/media contacts the most; government the least
  • Repeat check: babyshop.no rose to 101 requests on Table 3; we keep Table 2 (45) for ranking

Part 2 - ICO in plain language

  • Four purposes match: Skatteetaten tax data, Netflix streaming, fotball.no match history, babyshop.no checkout
  • Two consent tests are Partial: Skatteetaten optional cookies and document.no Google Signals
  • Partial means: the notice says consent, but the ICO marks consent INCONCLUSIVE - the wording is not clear enough, not that the site is automatically unlawful

7. If someone asks a quick question

Why does fotball.no rank highest but have no cookies? Because Webbkoll counts requests (calls to other hosts), not cookies. They are separate.

What does Partial mean? The notice points to consent, but the ICO tool cannot mark consent as valid for that purpose - usually because the wording is too vague or hidden.

Is Skatteetaten illegal? No. Tax data looks lawful. Only the optional cookie consent wording is uncertain in the ICO test.

Why two Skatteetaten rows? Tax data and optional cookies are two different purposes and need two ICO runs.
