Introduction

Group Assignment 1A has two parts. Part 1 maps third-party data sharing, cross-border contact and tracking on the 18 course-listed web services with Webbkoll. Part 2 (Group 1-A) uses the ICO lawful basis tool on five services chosen from that list. The 18 sites are Table 1. The five ICO sites are Table 1a. The Webbkoll numbers are Tables 2 and 3. Sector graphics are Figures 2 to 4.

The General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) sets the rules for personal data in the EEA (General Data Protection Regulation, 2016). Processing is lawful only if at least one Article 6 basis applies. The bases used here are legal obligation, public task, contract, consent and legitimate interests. Cookies are also covered by ePrivacy rules (Cookie Directive, 2009; ePrivacy Directive, 2002). A third-party request is a call from the page to another host. That is not the same as a third-party cookie.

Method

Webbkoll is a public scanner (5th of July Foundation, n.d.-c). It reports HTTPS, cookies, third-party requests and the server IP. It does not read a privacy notice, click a banner, or decide Article 6. The English interface was used on every service in Table 1. Figure 1 shows the check page. Each result is one live Chromium visit with no add-ons, no Do Not Track and no consent click (5th of July Foundation, n.d.-a, n.d.-b). The scan time is recorded with the result.

Server location is the Country field in KeyCDN IP Location Finder (KeyCDN, n.d.). Where no country was returned, the cell is marked not available. Norway is excluded from the country count. Internal cookies are set by the site. External cookies are set by other parties. Third-party requests count other hosts contacted when the page loads. A site can contact many hosts without setting a third-party cookie. Table 2 is the first measurement. Table 3 is a repeat measurement. The two series are not averaged.

Table 2 records the first measurement on 20 August 2026 for all 18 services in Table 1. Table 3 records a repeat measurement the same day, with KeyCDN countries for third-party hosts on 21 August 2026. A later visit can differ because Webbkoll stores a result for 24 hours.

Seventeen of 18 sites set zero third-party cookies. The exception is ikea.no (two cookies) after redirect to ikea.com. Third-party requests in Table 2 range from 1 (lanekassen.no) to 113 (fotball.no). Figure 2 ranks those counts. Figure 3 shows each site's share of the total. Figure 4 is the graphical overview per sector. News, media and entertainment contact the most hosts. Government sites contact the fewest. Spare time / sports is high because of fotball.no. It can only show that a host in that country was contacted (General Data Protection Regulation, 2016, art. 44-47).

Group 1-A: lawful basis for the five chosen services

From the 18 Webbkoll sites we chose five for the ICO tool (Table 1a): babyshop.no, skatteetaten.no, netflix.no, document.no and fotball.no. A service does not have one lawful basis for everything it does. The controller must name a purpose, then name a basis for that purpose (Information Commissioner's Office, n.d.). Each run states one concrete activity. Official ICO reports are dated 26 August 2026 (Information Commissioner's Office, 2026). Table 5 is the summary.

Article 6 is not a cookie click

Does a Yes/No banner decide whether the Tax Administration may process income data? No. Article 6 answers why personal data may be processed (General Data Protection Regulation, 2016, art. 6). Cookie rules answer whether information may be stored on a device (ePrivacy Directive, 2002). Mix the two in one ICO run, and the tool returns the wrong basis. For tax and the National Population Register, the notice says processing is required by law and is generally not optional (Norwegian Tax Administration, n.d.-a). The ICO report marks legal obligation and public task APPROPRIATE. Consent is NOT APPROPRIATE and likely invalid: the controller cannot offer a genuine free and ongoing choice for a statutory duty. Optional statistics cookies (Skyra, Matomo, Google Analytics, Siteimprove) are a different purpose. They use a Yes/No banner. A dedicated ICO run marks consent INCONCLUSIVE: the banner offers a choice, but the request does not fully name controller, purpose and types. No basis is APPROPRIATE for that cookie purpose.

The other four runs keep one purpose. Netflix needs account and payment data to provide the paid service (Netflix, 2026). Contractual necessity means the processing is needed to perform the agreement, not merely useful. ICO marks contract APPROPRIATE and consent as likely invalid: there is no real, ongoing choice if the service cannot be delivered without the data. Norges Fotballforbund publishes name and club of active players aged 13+ as match history, with opt-out through the club (Norges Fotballforbund, n.d.). ICO marks legitimate interests APPROPRIATE. Membership in FIKS may rest on contract. Cookiebot may rest on ePrivacy consent (ePrivacy Directive, 2002). Those are other purposes. Babyshop's sales terms are a purchase contract (Babyshop Sthlm Holding AB, n.d.). ICO marks contract APPROPRIATE for checkout. The notice does not label Article 6(1)(b). Marketing and cookies stay separate.

document.no is the case that fails the consent test. Google Signals is described as advertising analytics. Article 6 is not named (Document Media AS, n.d.). Collection is said to run only with a Google login and ad personalization. That looks like consent. The ICO report marks no basis APPROPRIATE. Consent is INCONCLUSIVE because a valid request must be clear, prominent and separate from other terms. The choice sits in Google settings. Document Pluss terms also bundle acceptance of the privacy notice. Contract and legitimate interests are not appropriate for this advertising purpose. A notice may aim at consent and still fail the request.

Optional cookies after No

Webbkoll does not click banners (5th of July Foundation, n.d.-b). skatteetaten.no/person/ was opened in a clean browser and No was selected on 24 August 2026. That tests whether optional tracking is blocked after rejection. It is not the lawful basis for tax administration. Conclusion

The 18 course-listed sites are in Table 1. Webbkoll numbers for all of them are in Tables 2 and 3. Figure 4 is the sector overview. The worst data sharer on request volume is fotball.no. The widest country list is document.no. The most restrained e-government sites are lanekassen.no and altinn.no. The five ICO services (Table 1a) are babyshop.no, skatteetaten.no, netflix.no, document.no and fotball.no. Four of five core purposes match the notice (Table 5). Two consent tests fail: optional cookies on skatteetaten.no and Google Signals on document.no. A legal duty to assess tax is not a cookie click. One purpose requires one basis.
