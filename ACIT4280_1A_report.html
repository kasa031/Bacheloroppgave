Introduction

This report answers two questions. When a person opens a Norwegian-facing front page, how many other companies does that page contact, and in which countries do those machines appear to sit? When a site uses personal data, has it named a GDPR Article 6 basis,1 and does that basis hold in the ICO tool? The questions differ. A news site can load many ad scripts and still have a contract for a paid account. A tax office can lawfully hold income data and still fail a consent test for optional statistics cookies. Part 1 measures contact. Part 2 tests one purpose at a time.

Table 1 lists the 18 course sites. Webbkoll scanned all 18.2 Table 1a lists five for the ICO tool.3 Counts: Tables 2 and 3. Sectors: Figures 2 to 4. ICO: Table 5.

Key terms

Personal data identifies a person (name, email, online identifier) (General Data Protection Regulation, 2016). The GDPR is the EU/EEA rule for that information and applies in Norway. Processing is collecting, storing, showing or sending it. It is lawful only if an Article 6 basis fits the purpose (General Data Protection Regulation, 2016, art. 6): legal obligation, public task, contract, consent, or legitimate interests.

A cookie is a small file on the device. Internal (first-party) cookies come from the site opened. External (third-party) cookies come from someone else, such as an advertiser.

A third-party request is a call from the page to another host (fonts, ads, video).5 It is not a third-party cookie. A page can call many hosts and set zero such cookies. We rank the 18 sites on requests (Table 2).

Part 1: How much does each site contact others?

What Webbkoll is

Webbkoll is a free scanner (5th of July Foundation, n.d.-c). It opens the URL in a clean Chromium session6 and reports HTTPS,7 cookies, third-party requests and the server IP8 (5th of July Foundation, n.d.-a, n.d.-b). Figure 1 is the start page. It does not read the privacy notice or decide Article 6. It records what one load contacted.

How we ran the scans

English Webbkoll on every Table 1 name. One live visit: no add-ons, no Do Not Track.9 Two visits on 20 August 2026. Table 2 is the first visit and the ranking. Table 3 is the later visit. We do not average them.

What KeyCDN is, and what a country cell means

KeyCDN IP Location Finder returns a likely country and provider for an IP address (KeyCDN, n.d.).10 "Location of server" uses the site IP. "List of countries" uses the third-party IPs Webbkoll listed. We count unique countries after dropping Norway. n/a means no country returned.

What each Webbkoll column means

Table 2 is one homepage load. Internal cookies: set by the site. External cookies: set by others. Third-party requests: calls to other hosts. Countries excl. Norway: unique countries of those hosts after dropping NO. The list may still print NO; the count already excludes it.

What Table 2 and Table 3 show

Part 2: Does the site have a lawful reason for this use of data?

What the ICO tool is

The ICO is the UK data protection authority. Its lawful-basis tool marks which Article 6 bases look APPROPRIATE for one activity (Information Commissioner's Office, 2026).14 We used the official Word report (26 August 2026). Name a purpose, then a basis (Information Commissioner's Office, n.d.). Tax data, optional cookies and checkout are different purposes. Mix them, and the tool mixes the answers. Each Table 5 row is one purpose from Table 1a.

skatteetaten.no: tax data versus optional cookies

The notice names two purposes. Mixing them in one ICO run mixes the bases. Identity, income and tax data are processed because the law requires it; opt-out is generally not possible (Norwegian Tax Administration, n.d.-a). ICO: legal obligation and public task APPROPRIATE. Consent NOT APPROPRIATE (likely invalid): there is no free, ongoing No to a statutory duty.

Optional statistics cookies (Skyra, Matomo, Google Analytics, Siteimprove) are the second purpose named in the notice. The notice claims consent. ICO: consent INCONCLUSIVE (controller, purpose and types not fully named). No basis APPROPRIATE. Table 5 has two rows. Figure 7 is the hub that already splits the privacy notice from the cookie page.

netflix.no: paid streaming

Purpose: account and payment data needed to provide the paid subscription (Netflix, 2026). ICO: contract APPROPRIATE. Consent likely invalid: no real choice if the service cannot run without the account. Ads and marketing in the same notice are other purposes and were left out.

fotball.no: name and club on the match list

Norges Fotballforbund publishes name and club of active players aged 13+, with club opt-out (Norges Fotballforbund, n.d.). ICO: legitimate interests APPROPRIATE. NFF is not a public authority, so public task does not fit. FIKS membership may rest on contract.15 That is another purpose and was not this ICO run.

document.no: Google Signals

Google Signals is advertising analytics.16 Article 6 is not named (Document Media AS, n.d.). Collection is said to run only with a Google login and ad personalization. That looks like consent. ICO: no basis APPROPRIATE. Consent INCONCLUSIVE: the request is not clear, prominent and separate; the choice sits in Google settings; Document Pluss terms also bundle the notice. Contract and legitimate interests do not fit this advertising purpose. Table 5: Partial.

babyshop.no: checkout

Purpose: name, address, contact, order and payment to deliver goods. The sales terms are a purchase contract (Babyshop Sthlm Holding AB, n.d.). ICO: contract APPROPRIATE. The notice does not label Article 6(1)(b). Marketing stays a separate purpose.

How to read Table 5

Each row is one purpose. Yes: notice and ICO mark line up. Partial: the notice aims at consent; ICO marks consent INCONCLUSIVE and no basis APPROPRIATE.

Conclusion

Part 1: fotball.no has the most third-party requests (113) and zero third-party cookies. document.no maps to the most countries. lanekassen.no and altinn.no are the quietest e-government fronts. News/media is the highest sector; government the lowest. A request count is not a cookie count.

Part 2: four core purposes match the notice (tax, Netflix, match lists, checkout). Two consent tests fail: skatteetaten optional cookies and document.no Google Signals. One purpose needs one basis. Together the two parts show how the page loads, and why the controller may use the data.
